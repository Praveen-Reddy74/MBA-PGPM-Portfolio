# Reducing E-Waste through Effortless Reuse of Electronic Devices in Delhi NCR

**Term:** 5 · **Subject:** DT — Design Thinking · **Professor:** Mr. Anand Shankar (confirmed in the original report; matches subject workbook)
**Type:** Group project (Design Thinking process report) · **Status:** Group Project — 6 members

## Objective
Design a low-cost, low-effort way for Delhi NCR households to reuse old but functional electronic devices (phones, tablets, laptops) instead of letting them become e-waste.

## Approach — Design Thinking process
- **Empathize:** Identified target customer segments (middle-income families, parents of school-going children, students, elderly users) and built a persona ("Neha," a working mother in Noida) to ground the problem in a real user scenario. Surfaced key emotional barriers: fear of data misuse during repair/resale, perceived repair cost/complexity, and low technical confidence.
- **Define:** Framed the core problem statement — how to enable households to reuse old devices with minimal cost, effort, and technical know-how.
- **Ideate:** Generated multiple concepts, including a "Second-Life Repurposing Framework" (repurposing old devices as IoT dashboards, digital photo frames, or home servers), a printed/QR-based "Device Revival Guide," community reuse drives, a student-volunteer support model, and a "Linux Learning Laptop Program" donating refurbished laptops to underprivileged students.
- **Prototype:** Outlined a Student Volunteer Support Model, training students (as part of their own curriculum) to assist households with device setup and revival.

## Output
A written Design Thinking process report covering all four stages, from empathy research through a prototyped service concept.

## What this demonstrates
End-to-end application of the Design Thinking methodology to a real sustainability/social-impact problem, from qualitative empathy work through to a concrete, low-cost service prototype.

## Academic context
Graded group coursework for Term 5's Design Thinking course.
