# Means-End Chain — Sustainability in Food Consumption

**Term:** 3 · **Subject:** MR — Marketing Research · **Professor:** Dr. Harish Kumar (confirmed in the original report; matches subject workbook) · TA: Mr. Aaqib Danish
**Type:** Group project (qualitative research report) · **Status:** Group Project — 6 members

## Objective
Explore how students interpret "sustainable food" (organic, local sourcing, eco-friendly packaging) and uncover the underlying personal values driving their consumption choices.

## Approach
- Exploratory qualitative research design using the Means-End Chain (MEC) framework, which links product attributes to consequences and ultimately to personal values.
- Purposive sample of 5 respondents from the PGPM cohort.
- Data collected via laddering interviews (10–20 minutes each), probing progressively from concrete food choices toward terminal values (health, environment, identity).
- Interview transcripts coded and categorized to build the means-end chain.

## Output
A written research report covering rationale, methodology, and MEC-based analysis of student attitudes toward sustainable food consumption.

## What this demonstrates
Applied qualitative consumer-research methodology (laddering / Means-End Chain analysis) — a less common but rigorous approach to understanding the "why" behind consumer choices, beyond survey-based methods.

## Academic context
Graded group coursework for Term 3's Marketing Research course.
