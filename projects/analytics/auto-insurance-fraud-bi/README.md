# Auto-Insurance Fraud — Business Intelligence Analysis

**Term:** 3 · **Subject:** BI — Business Intelligence · **Professor:** Dr. Sanchita Kuchi (per subject workbook)
**Type:** Group project (BI/dashboard-oriented analysis) · **Status:** Group Project — 5 members

## Objective
Analyze an auto-insurance claims dataset (1,000 policies, 39 columns) to understand fraud patterns and identify what a BI dashboard should surface for stakeholders monitoring claims.

## Approach
- Reviewed dataset structure: policy/customer information, customer profile (demographics, occupation, hobbies), incident details, claims data, vehicle information, and a binary fraud label.
- Framed the business problem: insurers face financial losses from fraudulent claims, and BI dashboards can help monitor claims, detect fraud patterns, and optimize premiums/deductibles.
- Identified key business questions: which customer profiles, policy types, and incident characteristics are most indicative of fraud.

## Output
A presentation covering data structure, business context, and key questions a fraud-monitoring BI dashboard should answer.

## What this demonstrates
Framing a raw dataset in business-intelligence terms — translating a fraud-detection dataset into concrete dashboard and monitoring requirements, not just a modeling exercise.

## Academic context
Graded group coursework for Term 3's Business Intelligence course.
