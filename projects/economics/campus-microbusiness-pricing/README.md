# Campus Micro-Business — Pricing & Demand Analysis

**Term:** 1 · **Subject:** BE1 — Business Economics-I · **Professor:** Prof. Vikas Prakash (per subject workbook)
**Type:** Group project (coursework report) · **Status:** Group Project — 6 members

## Objective
Apply microeconomic pricing and demand-estimation concepts to a real, small-scale business: a two-item campus food stall (samosas and rasmalai) selling to students and faculty.

## Context
Run as a hands-on Business Economics exercise: the group operated an actual micro-business on campus for a short period, using it as a live case for demand estimation, revenue analysis, and profit-maximization concepts.

## Approach
- Defined the business objective (profit maximization under a limited campus food-license budget) and identified real operating constraints (capital, licensing cost, day-wise demand uncertainty).
- Collected primary demand data from prospective customers (~190-person estimated potential base) at a range of price points.
- Built a market demand schedule across multiple candidate price points and plotted the implied demand curve.
- In a second report, defined and computed Total Revenue, Average Revenue, and Marginal Revenue schedules for the product line.

## Output
A two-part written report: Report 1 (business setup, constraints, demand estimation, demand curve) and Report 2 (revenue schedules and marginal analysis).

## What this demonstrates
Practical application of demand estimation and revenue/marginal-analysis frameworks to a real, small operating business rather than a purely theoretical exercise.

## Academic context
This is a graded group coursework submission for Term 1's Business Economics-I course at Great Lakes Institute of Management (PGPM program).
